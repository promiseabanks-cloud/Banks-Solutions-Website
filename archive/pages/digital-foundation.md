THE PROBLEM

## You can usually tell within a minute.

These are the symptoms we hear most often. If two or more sound familiar, the problem is structural, not cosmetic.

**It gets visitors but no enquiries.** People arrive, look, and leave without doing anything. Traffic was never the problem.

**You avoid sending people to it.** When someone asks for your website, you hesitate. That hesitation is the whole diagnosis.

**It no longer matches the business.** You have grown, raised your prices, changed direction, and the site still describes who you were three years ago.

**It is slow on a phone.** Which is where more than eight in ten of your visitors are.

**Nobody can update it.** A change means calling whoever built it, if they’re still reachable.

**You cannot find it on Google.** Searching your own business name turns up your competitors first.

WHY IT MATTERS

## You are being judged before the conversation starts.

By the time someone contacts you, they have already made most of the decision. They found you through a search, a referral or a forwarded link, spent under a minute on your site, and formed a view about how established you are, how competent you are, and roughly what you charge.

None of that is about how attractive the site is. It is about whether it answered the questions they arrived with: what you do, who you do it for, whether anyone like them has trusted you before, and what happens if they get in touch.

This is why we do not start with design. We start with the decision the visitor is trying to make, work out what they need in order to make it, and build the structure that gets them there. Design comes last, because how something looks can only support a decision the structure has already set up.
