THE PROBLEM

## The failures that cost most are the quiet ones.

Nobody notices a neglected website until the day it matters. By then it is an emergency and it is expensive.

**Nobody actually owns it.** The developer who built it moved on. Nothing has been updated since.

**Backups exist in theory.** Nobody has ever restored one to check they work, which is the only test that counts.

**It went down and you found out from a customer.** There was no monitoring, so nobody knew.

**It has got slower and nobody noticed.** Performance decays gradually, so it never triggers an alarm.

**Small changes take weeks.** Updating a phone number means finding someone who’s available.

**Security is a hope, not a plan.** Out-of-date plugins are the most common way small business sites get compromised.

WHY IT MATTERS

## A website nobody maintains is a liability with a countdown on it.

This is the part of the industry that gets sold badly. Agencies build a site, invoice, and disappear, and the client is left with a piece of business infrastructure nobody is responsible for. It works fine for a while, which is exactly why nobody worries about it.

Then something happens. A plugin update breaks the contact form and enquiries silently stop arriving for three weeks. The hosting bill fails and the site disappears mid-campaign. Someone exploits an old plugin and Google flags you as unsafe, which takes far longer to undo than to prevent.

None of these are dramatic. All of them are avoidable with someone doing routine, boring, unglamorous work every month. That is what this service is.
