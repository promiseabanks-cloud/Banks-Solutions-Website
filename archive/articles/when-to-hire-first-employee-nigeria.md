# When Should You Hire Your First Employee? (And What Should They Actually Do?)

> You're the one answering every message, packing every order, and chasing every payment. The business is growing, technically — but so is the pile of things only you can do. That's usually the real signal it's time to hire, not a date on a calendar.

Business owners tend to overthink this decision in one specific way: they wait for a number. A revenue target, a savings cushion, some external permission slip that says now is officially the time. In practice, the signal that actually matters shows up long before any of that.

![Nigerian entrepreneur sitting overwhelmed at a cluttered desk](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/solo-nigerian-entrepreneur-overwhelmed-desk.jpg)

## The real signal isn't revenue

Plenty of business owners wait for a specific revenue number before they consider hiring, as if there's a magic threshold. The more reliable signal is simpler: your own time has become the actual bottleneck on revenue-generating work. If you're spending hours a day on tasks that don't grow the business — repetitive admin, routine replies, packing and labelling — while the work that actually brings in money sits waiting for your attention, that's the bottleneck showing up clearly.

## Occasional overflow vs. consistent overflow

One busy week doesn't mean you need staff — it means you had a busy week. What matters is whether that overflow has become the normal state rather than the exception. If you're consistently working late just to stay level, not to get ahead, that consistency is the real signal, not any single stretched moment.

## Why this decision matters so much

Poor employment practices and inexperienced staffing decisions are named among the top reasons small businesses in Nigeria fail, according to SMEDAN-cited research. Hiring too early, hiring for a vague role instead of a specific need, or hiring the wrong person for the actual bottleneck can turn a growth decision into the thing that sinks you. This isn't a decision to rush just because you're tired.

## Define the role around one bottleneck

Don't hire for vague "help around the business." Pick the single task or category of task that's actually eating your time and costing you revenue-generating hours, and build the role specifically around solving that. A role built around one clear, specific bottleneck is easier to hire for, easier to measure, and easier for the new person to actually succeed at than a vague, sprawling job description nobody — including you — can fully define.

## Once you've decided to hire

The operational decision of when and who is separate from the compliance side — NSITF registration, pension thresholds, minimum wage rules — which is its own detailed topic worth reading before you actually bring someone on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a specific revenue number that means I should hire?

Not really — the more useful signal is whether your own time has become the bottleneck on work that actually generates revenue, regardless of the exact revenue figure.

### What if I'm just going through a busy season?

A single busy stretch isn't the signal — consistent overflow that's become your normal state, not the exception, is what actually points to needing help.

### Should I hire someone for general support first?

It's usually better to hire around one specific, well-defined bottleneck than a vague "general help" role — it's easier to hire well for and easier for the person to succeed at.

### What do I need to handle once I've decided to hire?

The statutory side — NSITF registration from your first hire, pension thresholds, and minimum wage rules — is a separate, detailed compliance topic worth reading on its own.
