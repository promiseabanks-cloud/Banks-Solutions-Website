# The Real List of Grants and Loans Nigerian Business Owners Can Actually Apply For

> Somebody's always sharing a link to a grant that supposedly gives out free money if you fill a form. Most of it isn't real, or isn't current, or isn't for a business like yours. Here's what actually exists right now.

This isn't a "10 grants you didn't know about" list built for clicks. It's the real, currently operating programmes Nigerian business owners can genuinely look into — with the honest caveat upfront: eligibility, sector focus, and application windows change, sometimes without much notice, so treat this as a starting point for your own research, not a guarantee.

![Close-up of a man's hand holding a fan of Nigerian naira banknotes](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-naira-business-grant-loan.jpg)

## Government-backed programmes

SMEDAN offers ₦50,000 in financial relief to nano and micro businesses, and it's structured to reach all 774 local governments — worth checking regardless of where you're based. The BOI Youth Entrepreneurship Support (YES) Programme goes further, offering loans up to ₦10 million along with training and mentorship, aimed at agriculture, manufacturing, tech, healthcare, education, and the creative industry.

AGSMEIS, delivered through NIRSAL Microfinance Bank, runs at 9% interest per annum with no collateral required for smaller loans, targeting agriculture, manufacturing, education, hospitality, and creative businesses. There's also the ₦75 billion MSME Fund, offering loans up to ₦1 million at roughly the same 9% rate.

## Non-government options worth knowing

The Tony Elumelu Foundation Entrepreneurship Programme is one of the most established of its kind on the continent, and worth applying to even if your odds feel long — it runs annually and reaches thousands of applicants. The Africa Enterprise Challenge Fund (AECF) focuses on agriculture, renewable energy, climate, and financial inclusion, so it's a better fit for businesses in those specific spaces than a general retail or service business.

MarketMoni and TraderMoni offer interest-free loans up to ₦100,000 — smaller amounts, but genuinely interest-free and aimed at the smallest end of the business spectrum, where even a modest amount of working capital can matter a lot.

## The honest part

None of these are guaranteed approvals, and the details above can shift — interest rates, loan caps, and which sectors get priority all move over time. Before you apply to any of them, go to the actual programme's current page and confirm the numbers, the deadline, and the eligibility criteria yourself. A list like this tells you where to look. It shouldn't be the last thing you read before you apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which of these is easiest to qualify for?

MarketMoni and TraderMoni tend to have the lowest barrier to entry given the smaller loan amounts, but eligibility still depends on the specific criteria at the time you apply.

### Do I need collateral for any of these?

AGSMEIS specifically waives collateral for smaller loan amounts — check each programme individually, since this varies.

### Is the Tony Elumelu Foundation programme only for tech businesses?

No — it's open across sectors, which is part of why it's so competitive. It's worth applying to regardless of your industry.

### How often do these details change?

Often enough that you should verify directly with each programme before applying — interest rates, caps, and sector focus aren't fixed permanently.
