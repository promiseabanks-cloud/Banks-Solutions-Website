# What Is a CRM, and Does Your Nigerian Business Actually Need One?

> No jargon — what a CRM actually is, what it does day to day, and how to tell if your business genuinely needs one yet.

“CRM” sounds like something only big companies with sales departments need. It's not. Strip away the jargon and a CRM is just this: one place where every customer and every conversation with them lives, instead of scattered across WhatsApp, your memory, and a notebook.

![What is a CRM Nigeria](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/what-is-a-crm-nigeria.jpg)

That's it. Not complicated software for tech companies — a system that answers the question “what's going on with this customer?” in five seconds instead of twenty minutes of searching.

## What it actually does, day to day

A new lead messages you on Instagram — it's logged automatically, instead of living only in your DMs where it'll eventually get buried.

It reminds you (or does it automatically) to follow up with someone who went quiet, instead of relying on you remembering.

Anyone on your team can see a customer's history — what they asked, what they bought, what was promised — without calling you to ask.

## What a CRM is not

It's not a replacement for actually talking to customers — it just makes sure nothing said gets lost. It's not automatically “AI” or complicated — the underlying idea (one record per customer) is decades old. And it's not one-size-fits-all software — a CRM configured around a generic template rather than your actual process usually gets abandoned within a few months.

## Do you actually need one yet?

Ask yourself honestly: Do you ever lose track of who you're supposed to follow up with? If you were unreachable for a week, could your business still handle customers properly? Do you find yourself scrolling back through old WhatsApp chats trying to remember what you told someone?

If you answered yes to any of those, the honest answer is: yes, you need one — not because it's trendy, but because you're already paying the cost of not having it, just in a way that doesn't show up on a bill.

## Common CRM terms, in plain English

**Pipeline** — the stages a lead moves through, from first contact to paying customer. **Lead** — anyone who's shown interest but hasn't bought yet. **Automation / workflow** — a rule that does something for you automatically, like sending a reminder. None of this is more complicated than it sounds.

## Frequently asked questions

### Isn't this just for big companies?

No — modern CRM tools are built for small teams; the “enterprise-only” reputation is outdated.

### Will it take long to set up?

A properly scoped setup (not a generic template) usually takes a few weeks, not months.

### What does it cost?

Setup for a properly connected system typically starts around ₦800,000, then a smaller monthly amount to keep it running and improving.

### Do I need one if I only have a handful of customers?

Probably not yet — a CRM earns its cost once volume or team size makes manual tracking the actual bottleneck.

### What's the difference between a CRM and just using Google Sheets with reminders?

A spreadsheet only reminds you if you remember to check it. A CRM triggers the reminder itself, and doesn't depend on one person's discipline.

### Can more than one person use it at the same time?

Yes — that's one of the main points of moving off a personal WhatsApp or notebook in the first place.
