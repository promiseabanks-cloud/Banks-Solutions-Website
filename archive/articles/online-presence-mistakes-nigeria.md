# 5 Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make With Their Online Presence (And How to Fix Them)

> The five most common, most costly mistakes Nigerian businesses make with their website and online presence — and exactly how to fix each one.

After looking closely at how Nigerian businesses actually show up online, the same five mistakes come up again and again — across industries, across business sizes. The good news: every one of them is fixable, usually without a huge budget.

![Online presence mistakes Nigeria](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/online-presence-mistakes-nigeria.jpg)

1. The website exists, but nobody's looked after it in years.

An outdated site is often worse than no site — it actively signals “this business might not be active anymore.” Fix: a proper maintenance plan, not a one-time build and disappear.

2. Leads come in, and then go nowhere.

Someone messages, fills a form, shows real interest — and gets no timely follow-up, because there's no system flagging it as urgent. Fix: connect your website to a real system that won't let a lead sit unanswered.

3. The Google Business Profile is empty, outdated, or doesn't exist.

This is often the very first thing a nearby customer sees — before your website, sometimes before they even know your name. Leaving it blank is turning away ready-to-buy customers for free. Fix: claim it, fill it in completely, and keep it current.

4. The website looks fine on a laptop and terrible on a phone.

Since the large majority of Nigerian internet users are on mobile, this single mistake quietly costs more businesses more customers than almost anything else on this list. Fix: test your site on an actual phone, not just imagine how it looks.

5. The copy talks about the business instead of the customer's actual problem.

“We provide innovative solutions for modern businesses” tells a visitor nothing. The businesses that convert visitors are the ones that name the actual problem the customer is dealing with, specifically, in the first few seconds. Fix: rewrite it around what changes for the customer, not what the business does technically.

## A sixth one, worth naming separately

No way to tell if any of this is working. Without at least basic tracking of where enquiries come from, a business can't tell which of these five mistakes is actually costing it the most — or whether a fix is working. This is usually the first thing worth putting in place, before spending money fixing the rest.

The pattern underneath all six: none of these are really “website problems.” They're all versions of the same thing — a digital presence that was set up once and then left alone, instead of treated as something that needs ongoing attention to keep working.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which of these should I fix first?

Whichever one is actively costing you customers right now — usually #2 (lost leads) or #3 (Google Business Profile) have the fastest, most visible payoff.

### Can these be fixed without rebuilding everything?

Yes, in most cases — these are fixable improvements, not reasons to start over.

### How do I find out which of these apply to my business?

A short audit is usually enough to spot which of these are real for you — you don't need to guess or fix all six blind.

### Is it normal to have more than one of these at once?

Very common — most businesses we look at have two or three, not just one, since they tend to compound over time.
