# Why 67% of Lagos Shoppers Won't Buy From an Unbranded Business

> You've got the product. Pricing's fair. Quality's actually good. And people still hesitate to buy from you online — not because of anything you're selling, but because your page looks like it could disappear tomorrow.

67% of Lagos consumers say they avoid buying from unbranded shops. Not unbranded as in "no logo" strictly — unbranded as in nothing that signals this is a real, accountable business rather than someone who might vanish with your money and your number.

![Man in traditional attire standing in a branded clothing store called Ruk-T Style House](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/branded-nigerian-boutique-shop-owner.jpg)

## Why Nigerians are skeptical by default

This isn't customers being difficult. It's a rational response to a market where internet fraud and failed banks have both been real, recent experiences for a lot of people. Trust has been called the most expensive currency in Nigeria, and that's not an exaggeration — it's why "radical transparency" has become a real trend in how Nigerian businesses present themselves, not a marketing buzzword someone invented.

## What actually builds trust (not what you think)

None of this requires a big budget. A professional logo — not necessarily an expensive one, just consistent and deliberate. The same colors, the same look, every time someone sees your business, instead of a different font and vibe on every post. An Instagram or social presence that looks organized rather than abandoned or chaotic. A clear bio that tells someone what you do and what to do next, instead of making them guess.

These are small, doable things. What kills trust is the absence of them, or worse, inconsistency — a slick logo on one post and a blurry screenshot the next.

## The myth worth pushing back on

There's a real, ongoing conversation in Nigerian business press about "SMEs and the myth of affordable branding" — the idea that looking legitimate requires money most small businesses don't have. It doesn't. What it actually requires is consistency and a bit of intention. What it can't survive is being absent altogether, or looking like nobody's paying attention to it.

You don't need a rebrand. You need your existing colors, logo, and bio to actually match across every place a customer might find you — and to stay that way.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an expensive logo to be taken seriously?

No — a simple, professional-looking logo used consistently does more for trust than an expensive one used inconsistently.

### Is Instagram enough, or do I need a website too?

Instagram is a good starting point for a lot of businesses, but a website adds a layer of permanence and legitimacy that a social page alone doesn't fully carry.

### What's the fastest thing I can fix this week?

Consistency — same colors, same logo, a clear bio, across every platform a customer might land on. It costs nothing but attention.

### Why does this matter more in Nigeria specifically?

Because customer skepticism here is higher by default, shaped by real experiences with fraud and failed institutions — trust has to be actively earned, not assumed.
