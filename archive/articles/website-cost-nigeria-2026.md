# How Much Does a Website Really Cost in Nigeria in 2026?

> Real price ranges for websites in Nigeria in 2026, what you actually get at each price point, and the hidden costs most people forget to ask about.

Ask five different people “how much does a website cost in Nigeria?” and you'll get five wildly different answers — ₦50,000, ₦300,000, ₦2,000,000. Nobody's necessarily lying. They're just talking about completely different things.

![Website design cost Nigeria 2026](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/website-cost-nigeria-2026.jpg)

Here's what the market actually looks like, honestly:

| Site type | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| Basic 3–5 page site | ₦120,000–₦300,000 |
| Site with a blog + lead capture | ₦250,000–₦600,000 |
| Real online store (browse + pay) | ₦400,000–₦1,200,000 |
| Custom web app / multi-system build | ₦2,500,000+ |

A basic 3–5 page site (the kind that just says what your business does): typically ₦120,000–₦300,000.

## A site with a blog and a way to capture leads: ₦250,000–₦600,000.

A real online store — where people can actually browse products and pay you: ₦400,000–₦1,200,000, depending on how complex it needs to be.

## What actually moves the price

Two quotes for what sounds like “the same website” can be three times apart because they're not actually the same job. The things that move the number most:

**Custom design vs. a template.** A template filled in with your logo and colours is faster and cheaper. A design built around how your specific business sells is slower and costs more — and usually converts better, which is a real trade-off, not a scam either way.

**Who writes the words.** If you're handing over finished copy, that's less work for whoever builds it. If they have to work out what to say about your business first, that's a real chunk of the project, and it should be priced as one.

**How many things have to talk to each other.** A site that just displays information is simple. A site that takes payment, updates stock, sends confirmation emails and reports sales is several systems wired together, and each connection is a place things can go wrong if it's rushed.

**Revisions and structure of the deal.** A fixed quote with two rounds of revisions included behaves very differently from an hourly rate with no ceiling. Ask which one you're being offered before you agree to anything.

## The costs people forget to ask about

Hosting and domain renewal — usually ₦80,000–₦200,000 a year, every year, forever. A “free” website someone builds you once still costs money to keep alive.

VAT — web design services in Nigeria carry 7.5% VAT. On a ₦500,000 project, that's an extra ₦37,500 people are sometimes surprised by.

SSL (the padlock icon) — this should not cost extra. Free SSL via Let's Encrypt is standard on any competent hosting setup in 2026. If someone quotes it as a separate paid line item, that's worth questioning.

Payment gateway fees, if you're selling online — Paystack and Flutterwave both charge a percentage per transaction (roughly 1.5% locally, capped per transaction), not a website cost exactly, but a real ongoing cost of taking money online that people forget to budget for.

What happens when something breaks. This is the big one nobody prices in. A website with no ongoing support is a ticking clock — eventually it breaks, gets outdated, or gets hacked.

## DIY builder, freelancer, or agency

|  | DIY builder | Freelancer | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical cost | ₦15,000–₦60,000/yr | ₦50,000–₦400,000 | ₦300,000–₦2,000,000+ |
| Speed | Days | 1–4 weeks | 2–12 weeks |
| Who's accountable if it breaks | You | Depends if they're still reachable | Usually contractually defined |
| Best fit | Testing an idea, very tight budget | Simple, well-defined brief | Business depends on the site working |

None of these is universally “right.” A DIY builder is a legitimate choice if you genuinely just need to exist online while you validate an idea. The mistake is picking DIY-builder pricing and expecting agency-level accountability, or vice versa.

## Why the cheapest option often costs more later

A rushed, template-based site built for ₦100,000 usually has no real search visibility built in, no one to call when it breaks, and often needs to be rebuilt within a year or two anyway. You don't save money by going cheap — you delay the real cost and pay interest on it.

## How long it should take

As a rough guide: a basic site typically runs 2–4 weeks once content is ready. A site with a blog and lead capture is closer to 4–6 weeks. A proper online store is usually 4–8 weeks, more if inventory or payment logic is complex. If a build is offered to you in 48 hours for anything beyond a single landing page, ask what corners that speed requires.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a cheaper website ever the right call?

Sometimes — if you genuinely just need something simple to exist online for now. Just go in knowing what you're not getting.

### Does the price include hosting?

Not usually, anywhere in the market — always ask separately.

### What makes an online store cost more than a basic website?

Payment integration, product pages, and everything that needs to work correctly when real money is changing hands.

### How long does it take to build a website in Nigeria?

Roughly 2–4 weeks for a basic site once your content is ready, 4–8 weeks for an online store. Anyone promising a proper multi-page build in 48 hours is cutting something.

### Do I need my business registered with CAC before getting a website?

No — you can launch a website under a trading name. You'll want registration in place before you're invoicing at scale or opening a business bank account, but it's not a website prerequisite.

### Can I pay for a website in installments?

Most freelancers and agencies in Nigeria work on a deposit plus milestone payments — commonly 50% upfront, balance on delivery. Ask upfront; it's a normal thing to negotiate.

### What's the difference between a website builder like Wix and a custom-built site?

A builder is faster and cheaper but you're renting the platform — limited design control, and migrating away later is genuinely painful. A custom build (commonly WordPress) costs more upfront but you own it outright and can move hosts freely.

### Do I have to pay extra for SSL (the padlock)?

You shouldn't. Free SSL is standard with any competent hosting setup in 2026. Treat a separate SSL charge as a question worth asking, not a normal line item.

### Paystack or Flutterwave for payments?

Both are solid, well-used in Nigeria, and charge similar transaction fees. The right one usually comes down to which integrations or payout speed suit your specific business, not a clear winner either way.

### Will my website automatically work well on mobile?

Only if whoever builds it actually tests on a phone on real mobile data, not just resizes a browser window. Over eight in ten visitors to a typical Nigerian business site are on mobile — ask to see it on a phone before you sign off.
