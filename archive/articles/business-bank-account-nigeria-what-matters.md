# Opening a Business Bank Account in Nigeria: What Actually Matters

> You've registered the business. Now you need an account to actually run it through. Before you walk into the nearest bank branch out of habit, it's worth knowing what actually separates a good business account from one that'll frustrate you every month.

Opening a business account feels like a formality until it isn't — until the app goes down on the day you need to pay a supplier, or a fee you never agreed to shows up on your statement. Getting this choice right upfront saves you from years of quiet frustration.

![Smiling Nigerian businessman in a suit holding multiple bank cards](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-business-owner-bank-cards.jpg)

## The complaints that come up constantly

Ask any Nigerian small business owner about their bank and you'll hear some version of the same list: surprise charges that show up with no clear explanation, settlement delays on POS or online payments that leave you waiting for money that's technically already yours, apps that go offline right when you're trying to check a balance or move funds, and loan products that exist on the website but are practically impossible to actually access. None of this is rare. It's close to the default experience.

## What's changed recently

Banks have started launching dedicated SME account products with more competitive fees, built specifically for businesses your size instead of treating you like a scaled-down corporate account. Fintech players — Moniepoint among them — have built genuinely useful merchant accounts around transaction history rather than the traditional relationship-banking model. Worth actually comparing both types before you default to whichever bank is closest to your shop.

## The account types and what they actually mean

An SME Current Account is built for regular day-to-day business transactions — this is what most small operators need. A Corporate Account is a heavier structure, usually more relevant once you're a registered limited liability company with more complex banking needs. A Domiciliary Account holds foreign currency — genuinely useful if you import goods or deal in dollars regularly, and close to irrelevant if you don't. Don't open one because it sounds more serious; open it because you actually need to hold forex.

## What to actually check before choosing

Fee transparency first — ask directly what a typical month costs you, not just the headline "free account" pitch. Check the bank or fintech's app reliability reputation before you commit; ask other business owners, not just the sales rep. And check compatibility with whatever POS or fintech tools you're already using — some setups integrate smoothly, others create a mess of manual reconciliation you didn't sign up for.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a Corporate Account if I just registered a business name?

Usually not — an SME Current Account is typically the right fit for a business name or sole proprietorship. Corporate Accounts suit more complex Ltd structures.

### When do I actually need a Domiciliary Account?

When you regularly import goods, pay suppliers in foreign currency, or need to hold dollars or other forex — not as a general "sounds more professional" upgrade.

### Are fintech accounts as reliable as traditional bank accounts?

Many now are, and some are more reliable for specific things like fast settlement — but reputation varies by provider, so check real user experiences, not just marketing claims.

### What's the biggest hidden cost to watch for?

Surprise charges that aren't clearly disclosed upfront — ask directly what a typical month of transactions will actually cost before you open the account.
