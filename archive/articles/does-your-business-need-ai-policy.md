# Does Your Business Need a Written AI Policy?

> 77% of small businesses using AI tools have no written policy for how those tools should be used. Here's what a one-page AI policy should actually cover for a small Nigerian business — no legal jargon required.

Most small businesses that use AI tools didn't sit down and decide how those tools should be used — they just started using them. That's normal. It's also how customer data ends up somewhere it shouldn't, or how an AI-drafted message goes out with something wrong in it, before anyone thought to set a rule.

![Business owner reviewing an AI policy on a laptop](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/does-your-business-need-ai-policy.jpg)

77% of small businesses currently using AI tools have no written policy governing that use. And separately, 45% of small business owners say they're worried that “too much AI” could harm their reputation. Put those two numbers together and the picture is clear: plenty of businesses are worried about getting this wrong, without having written down what “right” actually looks like.

## You don't need a legal document

A written AI policy for a business your size doesn't need to be drafted by a lawyer or run to ten pages. One page, in plain language, that your team can actually read and follow, does more good than a thorough document nobody opens.

## What it should actually cover

- **What can go into an AI tool.** Customer names, phone numbers, order details — decide explicitly what's fine to paste into a chatbot or AI assistant and what isn't.
- **Customer data rules.** If a tool touches customer information at all, it should be clear who's responsible for how that data is stored and who can access it. This overlaps directly with your obligations under the NDPA 2023.
- **Who approves AI-generated customer-facing content.** A draft reply or social post written with AI help should have a person actually read it before it goes out — every time, not just when someone remembers.
- **What AI shouldn't be used for.** Some decisions — a refund exception, a pricing dispute, anything with real consequences for a customer — should stay with a person, explicitly.

## Why this matters more than it seems

Without a written rule, the standard tends to drift to whoever's using the tool that day. One staff member might paste in more customer detail than another would. One might publish an AI draft without reading it twice. A short written policy removes the guesswork — not because anyone's being reckless, but because nobody's actually agreed on the line until it's written down.

## How to actually write one

Sit down for twenty minutes with the four points above and answer each one for your business, specifically. Share it with anyone who touches an AI tool on your behalf. Revisit it every few months as you add new tools — it should grow with how you actually use AI, not sit untouched from day one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a lawyer to write an AI policy?

No — a clear, one-page document in plain language covers what most small businesses actually need.

### What's the biggest risk of not having one?

Inconsistency — different people making different calls about what's okay to paste into a tool or publish without review, with no agreed line to point to.

### How does this relate to the NDPA 2023?

Directly, if any AI tool touches customer data — the NDPA sets legal obligations around that data, and your AI policy should reflect them in practical terms for your team.

### Should I write this before or after I start using AI tools?

Ideally before, but if you're already using them, write it now — it's never too late to put a clear rule in place.
