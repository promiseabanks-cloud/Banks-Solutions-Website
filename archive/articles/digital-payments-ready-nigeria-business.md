# Digital Payments Are Now the Default in Nigeria — Is Your Business Actually Ready?

> Walk into almost any shop in Lagos or Abuja today and you'll see a POS terminal before you see a cash register. Digital payments stopped being optional a while ago. The real question isn't whether you accept them — it's whether your setup can actually handle it when something goes wrong.

73% of Nigerian SMEs plan to expand their digital payment capabilities this year. That's not businesses dabbling in something new — that's most of the market treating digital payments as the operating standard, not an experiment.

![Customer tapping a bank card on a POS payment terminal](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigeria-pos-card-payment-transaction.jpg)

## The hardware barrier basically disappeared

Mastercard's QR-on-Card technology has already turned 1.8 million Android phones into contactless payment terminals for SMEs and gig workers across the country. That's a real shift — accepting card payments used to require dedicated hardware and real upfront cost; now, for a lot of small operators, it just requires a phone they already own.

## POS machines are now just part of the shop

It's genuinely rare to walk into a physical store in Lagos or Abuja without seeing a Moniepoint terminal somewhere near the till. OPay, Flutterwave, and PalmPay are all building out merchant and SME payment rails aggressively, competing for exactly this space. The infrastructure question has largely been answered — the tools exist, they're accessible, and most businesses already have at least one of them.

## Having a POS machine isn't the same as being ready

This is where most businesses stop, and it's not far enough. A POS terminal handles the transaction. It doesn't automatically reconcile that payment against the actual order it belongs to — plenty of small businesses are still doing that matching by hand, or not doing it consistently at all, which is exactly where mistakes and missed payments hide.

## What "actually ready" looks like

Reconciling payments against orders as a habit, not an occasional catch-up task, so nothing quietly falls through. Having more than one payment method as backup — POS network downtime is a real, common occurrence, and a customer standing in front of you with a failed transaction and no alternative is a lost sale, not just an inconvenience. And keeping your in-person and website payment setups consistent with each other, rather than running your physical POS and your Paystack or Flutterwave website checkout as two disconnected systems that never talk to each other.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need a backup payment method if my POS works fine?

POS network downtime happens more often than most owners expect — a backup method turns a failed transaction into a completed sale instead of a lost customer.

### What does it mean to "reconcile" payments against orders?

Matching each payment you receive to the specific order it belongs to, consistently, so nothing gets missed or double-counted — a habit, not a once-in-a-while cleanup.

### Should my website and physical store use the same payment provider?

Not necessarily the same provider, but they should work together consistently rather than as two disconnected systems you have to manage separately.

### Is it worth switching from a basic POS to phone-based QR payments?

If cost or hardware access has been a barrier, phone-based options like Mastercard's QR-on-Card are worth a look — they lower the barrier to accepting card payments significantly.
