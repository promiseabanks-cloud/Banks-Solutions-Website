# What Nigerian Labour Law Actually Requires When You Hire Your First Employee

> You're finally hiring your first employee. Before you agree on a salary, there's a registration you're legally required to make from day one — not once you hit some headcount, from your very first hire.

Most first-time employers assume the legal obligations kick in once they've got a "real" number of staff — five, ten, whatever feels like a proper team. That assumption is wrong in at least one important way, and it's worth knowing before you bring anyone on, not after.

![Shop worker standing inside a small, fully stocked Nigerian provisions store](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-small-shop-employee-first-hire.jpg)

## NSITF starts at employee number one

Registration with the Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund is mandatory from your first employee. No minimum headcount, no sector exemption, no "we're too small for that yet." You contribute 1% of monthly payroll, and it covers workplace injury compensation — which matters more than it sounds like it should, the first time someone actually gets hurt on the job and you realize what isn't covered.

## Pension kicks in later, at three employees

The Contributory Pension Scheme becomes mandatory once you have three or more employees, not before. When it applies, you contribute 10%, your employee contributes 8%, adding up to a minimum 18% of pensionable emoluments — that's basic salary plus housing and transport allowance, not just the base figure. If you're still at one or two staff, this one doesn't apply to you yet, but it's worth knowing the trigger point in advance rather than discovering it retroactively.

## The minimum wage detail most people miss

The national minimum wage is ₦70,000 a month, effective since July 2024. Here's the part that surprises a lot of small business owners: it doesn't apply to businesses with fewer than 25 workers. If you're a small operation, that's genuinely useful to know — not as a loophole to exploit, but as an accurate picture of what the law actually requires of a business your size, rather than the number everyone quotes without the exemption attached.

## Other deductions worth knowing about

NHF (National Housing Fund) and NHIS (National Health Insurance Scheme) are other statutory payroll considerations you'll come across as you formalize employment. They're worth being aware of, though the detail of each is its own conversation — this is a starting map, not the whole territory.

## The honest summary

NSITF from employee one. Pension from employee three. Minimum wage rules that shift depending on your headcount. None of this is exhaustive legal advice, and a genuinely complex hiring situation is worth a proper consultation — but this is the map most first-time employers wish someone had handed them before they started.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to register with NSITF if I only have one employee?

Yes — NSITF registration is mandatory from your very first employee, with no minimum headcount or sector exemption.

### When does pension contribution become mandatory?

Once you have three or more employees. Below that, the Contributory Pension Scheme doesn't apply yet.

### Do I have to pay the ₦70,000 minimum wage if I only have five staff?

The national minimum wage specifically exempts businesses with fewer than 25 workers, so it may not legally apply to you, depending on your exact headcount.

### Is this guide enough to handle my hiring on my own?

It's a solid starting map for straightforward hiring, but it isn't exhaustive legal advice — a genuinely complex situation deserves a proper consultation.
