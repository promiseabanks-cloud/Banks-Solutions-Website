# Is Business Insurance Actually Worth It for a Small Nigerian Business?

> A fire, a break-in, a delivery that never arrives — any one of these could genuinely end a small business overnight. And yet almost nobody in Nigeria carries insurance against them. Here's why, and which specific risks are actually worth covering first.

Nigeria's insurance penetration sits at around 1%. Not 1% of small businesses — 1% of the entire population, across every kind of insurance there is. For a country with tens of millions of small businesses, that number is remarkably close to zero.

![Nigerian shopkeeper standing in a fully stocked provisions store](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-shop-owner-stocked-store-insurance.jpg)

## Why almost nobody has it

The two most common reasons owners give are almost disarmingly honest: "I don't know where to start," and "insurance is too expensive." Underneath those sit some real trust problems — a reputation, fair or not, for insurers being slow or unreliable when it's actually time to pay out. Add a complicated claims process that discourages people from even trying, and a cultural instinct that leans on faith over paperwork — "God will protect us" — and you get a market where almost nobody bothers, even when the risk is genuinely real.

## Not "insure everything" — insure this

You don't need a policy for every conceivable risk, and trying to get one is part of why people give up before starting. Start with what would actually end your business if it happened tomorrow. Fire and theft covering your premises and stock is the obvious first one — a single bad night can wipe out inventory it took years to build. Public liability matters if customers physically visit your premises; one accident on-site can turn into a legal and financial problem you never budgeted for. If you ship products, goods-in-transit cover protects you against loss or damage on the way to the customer — genuinely relevant given how often deliveries go wrong in Nigeria.

## How to actually vet an insurer

Don't just compare premiums — that's comparing the cost without checking whether the product behind it actually works when you need it. Ask specifically about claims-payment history: how long payouts typically take, and whether other business owners you know or can find have actually been paid out, not just sold a policy. A cheaper premium from an insurer with a poor payout reputation isn't actually cheaper — it's a bet you're likely to lose exactly when you can least afford to.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need insurance if my business is very small?

The smaller your buffer for absorbing a loss, the more a single bad event can end the business — which is exactly why it's worth considering even at a small scale, starting with the cheapest, highest-impact cover.

### What should I insure first if I can only afford one policy?

Fire and theft covering your premises and stock is usually the highest-impact starting point for most small retail or storage-based businesses.

### How do I know if an insurer actually pays out claims reliably?

Ask directly about their claims-payment history and talk to other business owners who've actually filed a claim — don't rely on the premium price or the sales pitch alone.

### Is goods-in-transit insurance worth it if I use delivery riders?

Yes, if loss or damage during delivery would be a real financial hit — it's specifically designed for exactly that risk.
