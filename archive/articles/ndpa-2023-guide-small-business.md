# The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023 — What Small Businesses Actually Need to Do

> The Nigeria Data Protection Act replaced the old NDPR in 2023. Here's what it actually requires from a small or growing business, in plain terms.

A quick honest note before we start: this is general information to help you understand the basics, not legal advice. For anything with real legal weight, talk to a qualified lawyer.

![NDPA 2023 Nigeria data protection](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/ndpa-2023-guide-small-business.jpg)

If your business collects any customer information — names, phone numbers, emails, payment details, even just an online order form — this law applies to you, whether you're a five-person shop or a large company.

## What changed

Nigeria's data protection rules used to run on the NDPR from 2019. That was replaced by the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, a stronger, more enforceable law, backed by a real regulator — the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC). This isn't a small technicality: the NDPC has already issued serious fines to major companies for getting this wrong, including hundreds of millions of naira against one company and a very large fine against a global tech company. Enforcement is real, not theoretical.

## What this actually means for a small or growing business, practically

You need real, informed consent before collecting someone's personal data — not a vague “by using this site you agree” buried at the bottom of a page.

You need a clear privacy policy that's easy to find and actually explains what you do with customer data — not copy-pasted legal text nobody, including you, understands.

You need to actually protect the data you collect — reasonable technical and organizational security, not just a promise on paper.

If a data breach happens, there are notification obligations — this isn't something to figure out for the first time during an actual crisis.

The honest risk of ignoring this: it's not just a legal one. Customers are increasingly aware of data protection, and a business that's careless with their information loses trust fast — often faster than a competitor with a worse product but better handling of customer data.

Where this connects to your website and systems: every form on your site, every customer record in a CRM, every automated message — all of it touches this law. It should be built in from the start, not patched on afterward once something goes wrong.

## A basic starting checklist

A visible, plain-language privacy policy linked from every page that collects data. A clear opt-in (not a pre-ticked box) before adding someone to marketing messages. Knowing exactly where customer data is stored and who has access to it. A named person internally responsible for data questions, even in a small business. This is a starting point for the conversation with a lawyer, not a substitute for one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does this apply to me if I'm a small business, not a big company?

Yes — the law doesn't exempt businesses based on size.

### Do I need a lawyer for this?

For real compliance certainty, yes, eventually — this article gives you the practical basics, not a legal sign-off.

### Is this something Banks Solution handles when building a website or system?

We build with these basics in mind by default — proper consent, secure handling of data — though full legal compliance review is still a job for a qualified lawyer.

### What counts as “personal data” under this law?

Broadly, any information that can identify a person — name, phone number, email, address, and similar details, not just sensitive records like payment info.

### What if I'm already collecting data without any of this in place?

Start now rather than waiting — putting the basics in place going forward is far better than doing nothing while continuing to collect data.
