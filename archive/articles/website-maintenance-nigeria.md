# Website Maintenance in Nigeria — What It Actually Costs, and What Happens If You Skip It

> What website maintenance actually includes, what it costs in Nigeria, and what really happens to a business site that never gets looked after.

Most businesses treat their website like a signboard — put it up once, forget it exists. The problem is a website isn't a signboard. It's more like a car: it needs fuel, servicing, and someone paying attention, or it eventually strands you at the worst possible time.

![Website maintenance cost Nigeria](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/website-maintenance-nigeria.jpg)

## What actually happens to a website nobody maintains

Software behind the scenes goes out of date, which is one of the most common ways small business sites get hacked.

Nobody notices when it goes down — until a customer tells you, or worse, doesn't tell you and just leaves.

Small requests (“can we update our prices?”) pile up because there's no one accountable for handling them.

Performance quietly degrades — nobody notices a website getting slower month by month the way they'd notice it going down entirely, so it never triggers action.

## Warning signs you need this now

You don't know who currently hosts your website, or how to log in if you needed to.

Nobody has ever actually restored a backup to check it works — an untested backup is a hope, not a plan.

The plugins or theme haven't been updated in over a year (if it's WordPress, this is checkable in minutes and worth checking today).

The developer who built it is no longer reachable, or you'd have to think for a while before you could name them.

## What maintenance actually includes, done properly

Security monitoring, regular backups (so a disaster is a minor inconvenience, not a total loss), software updates, performance checks, and someone you can actually reach when something's wrong.

## Frequently asked questions

### My website hasn't broken in two years without maintenance — do I really need it?

You've been lucky so far. The risk doesn't show up until it does, and by then it's usually more expensive to fix than it would've been to prevent.

### Can I do basic maintenance myself?

If you're technical and have the time, sure. Most business owners don't have either to spare.

### What's the difference between maintenance and a rebuild?

Maintenance keeps what exists working. A rebuild is a new project — this is about avoiding needing one too soon.

### What if my site is already hacked or broken — can maintenance start there?

Yes. A takeover audit and stabilisation comes first: we fix or contain what's already wrong before any ongoing monitoring makes sense.

### How is this different from just paying for hosting?

Hosting keeps the site physically online. It does nothing about outdated plugins, untested backups, security, or someone actually watching for problems. They're not the same thing, despite sometimes being sold as if they are.

### How often are backups actually taken?

Automatic and off-site, on a regular schedule — and periodically restored to a test copy to confirm they actually work, which is the step most cheap setups skip.

### Can you take over maintenance from another developer or agency?

Yes — that's the normal starting point. We inventory what's currently in place, flag anything alarming, and stabilise before folding it into the regular cycle.

### Do I need this if my site is on a builder like Wix or Squarespace, not WordPress?

Less of it — those platforms handle core software updates for you. Backups, monitoring and someone accountable for changes still matter regardless of platform.

### Is a monthly retainer the only option?

It's what we recommend, because the value is in nothing ever being left to rot for months. One-off emergency fixes are also possible, but they cost more than prevention would have.
