# The Real Cost of 'I'll Just Use a Generator': Power Options for Nigerian Businesses in 2026

> You've probably said it yourself: 'the generator handles it.' Then you actually add up what the generator costs you in a month, and the number stops feeling like a minor operating expense.

Some businesses in Nigeria are spending over ₦1 million a week just to keep the lights and equipment running — fuel, maintenance, the generator itself wearing out faster than it should. That's not an outlier story. Unreliable electricity is estimated to cost the Nigerian economy $29 billion a year, something like 5 to 7 percent of GDP, according to World Bank figures. Power and access to finance are consistently named as the two biggest constraints Nigerian SMEs deal with — and most owners have made peace with the generator as just the cost of doing business, without ever really pricing out the alternative.

![Man repairing a small petrol generator outside a shop on a Nigerian street](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigeria-generator-repair-power-business.jpg)

## It's not "generator or full solar"

This is where most conversations about power go wrong. They present it as a binary — stay on diesel or go all-in on solar — when it's actually a spectrum, and most businesses should be moving along it, not jumping to the end of it.

## Where the spectrum actually starts

Better generator maintenance and fuel discipline is the cheapest place to start, and the one most businesses skip because it feels too small to matter. A poorly maintained generator burns more fuel and dies sooner — the fix costs almost nothing compared to what it saves. From there, a hybrid inverter-and-battery setup lets you run the essentials without the generator kicking in every time NEPA takes a break, which is usually where the fuel bill actually starts dropping in a way you notice.

## What full hybrid solar actually looks like

At the serious end, hybrid solar-generator systems have cut real businesses' energy costs by around 44% in actual deployments — and reduced generator runtime by 60 to 70%. A standalone setup sized for a small business or office typically runs ₦8,000,000 to ₦12,000,000. That's a genuinely big number for most small businesses, and it's fair to be honest about that rather than pretend it's a casual upgrade.

What's changed recently is the financing. Some vendors now offer models where they finance the equipment entirely, and you pay a blended tariff over a long-term contract — 10 to 20 years — instead of a huge upfront cost. It doesn't make solar free, but it does change the math for businesses that couldn't otherwise consider it.

## What to actually do with this

Don't feel pressured into a decision you can't afford yet. If ₦8 million-plus is nowhere near realistic right now, fix your generator discipline first — it's the step with the fastest, cheapest payoff, and it's available to you today. Look at hybrid inverter setups as the middle step once that's under control. Full hybrid solar is where you end up eventually, not where you have to start.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is solar actually cheaper than a generator long-term?

Often yes, over several years — but the upfront cost is real, which is why financing models that spread the cost matter for most small businesses.

### What's the cheapest way to cut power costs right now?

Generator maintenance and fuel discipline. It's the least glamorous fix and usually the one with the fastest payback.

### Do I need to go fully off-grid to benefit from solar?

No — a hybrid setup that reduces how often your generator runs still cuts costs meaningfully without requiring full independence.

### What does a vendor-financed solar deal actually involve?

The vendor covers the equipment cost and you pay a blended tariff over a long contract, often 10 to 20 years, instead of paying the full setup cost upfront.
