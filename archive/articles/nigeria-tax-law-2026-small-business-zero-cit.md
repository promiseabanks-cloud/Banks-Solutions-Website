# Nigeria's New Tax Law: Do You Really Owe ₦0 in Company Income Tax?

> A friend forwards you a WhatsApp message saying small businesses in Nigeria now pay zero tax. You run a small business. Does that mean you're covered? Mostly, yes — but not automatically, and not if you're in the wrong line of work.

Somebody in your business owners' WhatsApp group has probably already sent the message: "Good news — FG don cancel tax for small business." It's not wrong, exactly. It's just missing the parts that actually decide whether it applies to you.

![Nigerian business owner in a suit reviewing financial paperwork and using a calculator at his desk](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigeria-tax-law-2026-business-owner-review.jpg)

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025, in effect since January 1, 2026, does exempt a large number of small businesses from Company Income Tax. If your annual turnover is ₦100 million or below, and your fixed assets sit under ₦250 million, you're in the 0% CIT bracket. You're also exempt from Capital Gains Tax, from the 4% Development Levy, from VAT registration and collection, and from having withholding tax deducted on your income. Compare that to the 30% CIT that companies above the threshold still pay, and this is a real change, not just a headline.

## The part the WhatsApp message leaves out

The exemption isn't automatic. You don't get it simply by being small enough. You still need a Tax Identification Number. You still need to keep proper financial records — sales, expenses, whatever's relevant to your business. And you still have to file annual returns, even in a year your liability is ₦0.

Skip that filing and you haven't quietly avoided tax. You've just made yourself non-compliant with a liability you never actually proved was zero.

## Who doesn't qualify, no matter how small they are

There's a second catch, and it trips up more people than the paperwork does. A handful of professions are excluded from this exemption regardless of turnover: legal services, accounting and auditing, medical services, and consulting or advisory work. If that's your business, the ₦100 million ceiling doesn't help you — you're taxed under the standard rules whatever your size.

## What to actually do about it

Confirm you have a TIN. If you don't, get one — it's the entry point to all of this, exemption or not. Keep records that would hold up if anyone ever asked to see them. File your returns on time, including in the years you owe nothing, because the exemption only protects you if you can show you qualified for it.

None of that needs to be complicated for a business your size. It just needs to actually happen, instead of being assumed because someone forwarded you good news.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the ₦100 million threshold apply to profit or turnover?

Turnover — your total revenue for the year, not what's left after expenses. Your fixed assets also need to stay under ₦250 million.

### Do I still need to file taxes if I owe ₦0?

Yes. The exemption doesn't remove the filing requirement — it just means the amount due comes out to zero, and you need records to prove it.

### I run a consulting business under ₦100 million — am I exempt?

No. Consulting and advisory services are specifically excluded from this exemption, along with legal, accounting/auditing, and medical services, regardless of turnover.

### What happens if I don't get a TIN?

You can't properly claim the exemption, file returns, or prove your ₦0 liability without one — it's the starting point for all of this, not an optional extra.
