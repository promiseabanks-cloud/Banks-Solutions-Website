# 5 Legal Mistakes That Quietly Kill Nigerian Small Businesses

> Most business-ending legal mistakes in Nigeria aren't dramatic lawsuits. They're small, quiet oversights that sit unnoticed for years — until the exact moment they can't be undone anymore.

None of the five mistakes below involve a courtroom drama. They involve a document that never got signed, a step that felt optional at the time, or a line between personal and business money that quietly stopped existing. Each one is fixable in an afternoon. Each one is genuinely expensive to fix after the fact.

![Nigerian businessman signing a legal document at a table](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-business-owner-signing-legal-document.jpg)

## 1. Sharing real numbers with no NDA

A potential partner or investor asks to see your financials, your supplier list, your actual margins — and you send it over because the conversation feels friendly and promising. Without a signed confidentiality agreement first, you have no real protection if that conversation goes nowhere and your numbers end up informing someone else's competing business. Get a simple NDA signed before real financial detail changes hands, every time, regardless of how trustworthy the person seems.

## 2. Hiring with no actual employment contract

This is different from the statutory obligations around NSITF and pension — this is the document itself. A verbal understanding of role, salary, and expectations isn't a contract, and when a disagreement over pay, termination, or responsibilities eventually shows up, there's nothing specific to point back to. A basic written employment contract, signed by both sides before day one, closes this gap for the cost of an afternoon.

## 3. Operating without the license your activity actually requires

Certain activities need specific regulatory approval before you can legally operate — and it's easy to miss this if nobody flags it for you upfront, especially in food, health, finance-adjacent, or import/export businesses. Building months or years of operation on top of a missing license is a much more expensive problem to fix later than checking upfront ever would have been.

## 4. Never protecting your business name or trademark

Registering with CAC protects your business's legal existence. It doesn't automatically protect your brand name from a bigger, better-funded competitor legally using something close to it later. Trademark registration is the separate step that actually locks that down — skipped constantly by small businesses who assume CAC registration already covered it.

## 5. Mixing personal and business finances

Paying business expenses from your personal account, or pulling business revenue straight into personal spending with no clear line between the two — this is genuinely common, and it's a real legal and tax exposure, not just messy bookkeeping. It muddies your actual financial picture, complicates any future tax review, and can undermine the legal separation a registered company is supposed to give you. Separate accounts, separate discipline, from day one.

## The pattern across all five

Every one of these traces back to the same root cause: assuming a foreign or "normal" business practice applies locally, or assuming a general agreement covers something that actually needs its own specific document or approval. None of these require a lawyer on retainer to avoid. They require treating the paperwork as seriously as the business itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need an NDA for an informal conversation with a potential partner?

Yes, especially once real numbers or specific business details are being shared — "informal" conversations are exactly where this protection gets skipped and later regretted.

### Isn't CAC registration enough to protect my business name?

No — CAC registration establishes your business's legal existence, but trademark registration is the separate step that actually protects your brand name from being used by someone else.

### How do I know if my business needs a specific regulatory license?

Check requirements specific to your activity, especially in food, health, finance-adjacent, or import/export businesses — this is exactly the kind of thing that's easy to miss without someone flagging it directly.

### Why does mixing personal and business finances matter if I keep track of it mentally?

Because "mentally" isn't evidence — it creates real tax and legal exposure and undermines the financial separation a registered business is meant to provide.
