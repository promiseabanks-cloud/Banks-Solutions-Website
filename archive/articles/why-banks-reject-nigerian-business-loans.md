# Why Nigerian Banks Keep Rejecting Your Business Loan (And What Actually Works Instead)

> You applied for a business loan. Weeks later, a rejection with no real explanation, or worse, an approval you can't actually afford once you see the interest rate. You're not doing anything wrong. The system itself is the problem, and it's worth understanding why before you try again.

Nigeria's maximum lending rate hit 35.17% in 2026. Think about that number for a second — it means borrowing money to grow a business can cost more than the business itself will earn back in the same period. At that rate, a loan stops being a growth tool and starts being a gamble.

![Nigerian businessman looking worried while reviewing paperwork](https://bankssolution.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/img/nigerian-business-owner-bank-loan-rejected.jpg)

## Why banks say no so often

High interest rates are only part of it. Banks also want collateral most small business owners don't have, a credit history most people running a cash-based business never built, and paperwork that assumes you've already got the kind of financial records a five-person shop rarely keeps. None of that means your business is a bad bet — it means you don't fit the shape of borrower traditional banking was built around.

## The everyday friction on top of that

Even when you do get approved, the frustration doesn't stop there. Surprise charges you didn't see coming. Settlement delays on POS or online payments that leave you short right when you need the cash. Banking apps that go offline at the exact moment you're trying to move money. Loan products that technically exist but are practically impossible to actually access. Over 40 million MSMEs drive roughly half of Nigeria's GDP, and a huge number of them are dealing with exactly this, every month.

## What's actually changing

It's not all bad news. Banks are starting to launch dedicated SME products with more competitive fees, aimed specifically at businesses your size instead of treating you like a smaller version of a large company. Fintech lenders are doing something more interesting: offering working-capital loans based on your actual transaction history instead of collateral you don't have. Moniepoint is the clearest example — if you're already processing sales through their platform, that transaction data becomes your credit history, which is a fundamentally different starting point than walking into a bank with nothing but a business plan.

## What actually works instead

Before you apply anywhere, build a transaction history somewhere that counts — a POS provider, a fintech platform, anything that's tracking your real sales over time. When you do apply, apply to the lender whose product is actually built for a business your size, not the biggest bank on your street. And go in with real numbers, not hope: what you need the money for, exactly how it pays itself back, and over what timeline. That's the difference between a rejection and an approval more often than people expect.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do fintech lenders approve loans banks reject?

They often lend against your actual transaction history instead of collateral — if you're processing real sales through their platform, that data works as your credit history.

### Is a 35% interest rate normal?

It reflects Nigeria's maximum lending rate in 2026 — high enough that borrowing only makes sense if the return on what you're funding clearly beats that cost.

### Do I need collateral to get a business loan in Nigeria?

Traditional banks usually want it. Some fintech and government-backed programmes lend against transaction history or with reduced collateral requirements instead.

### What's the fastest way to improve my chances next time?

Build a real transaction history through a POS provider or fintech platform before you apply, and bring specific numbers showing exactly how the loan pays for itself.
