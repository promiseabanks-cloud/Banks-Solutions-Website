01 · The short version

Copy the 1904 Solutions recipe, then add a business on top

I looked inside the 1904 Solutions build to see exactly how we did it. It is a hand-coded WordPress theme with one template per page and a shared motion layer (GSAP, ScrollTrigger and Lenis smooth scroll), plus a separate "1904 Core" plugin that holds the customer app, wallet and Paystack payments. Elementor is still installed there but only one page uses it.

We do the same here, in two pieces:

  • The Banks theme draws everything people see: pages, motion, micro-interactions. It holds no business data, so it can be redesigned later without touching clients' projects.
  • The Banks Core plugin runs the business: onboarding, the portal, file uploads, progress tracking, invoices and payments, the retainer desk and product licences.

Banks Business Engine stays what it already is, its own plugin, and plugs into both.

What I found on the live site At least 38 published articles (served under Knowledge), a Glossary of 30 terms stored as its own content type, 12 or so service and info pages, Rank Math SEO data, WPForms and Elementor form entries, and Banks Business Engine running as a plugin with its own leads, customers, bookings, automations and licensing data. That list is the preservation checklist in the next section.

02 · Preserve first

Nothing gets deleted until we've proved we can restore it

The live site keeps running the whole time. We build on a staging copy and switch over in one move at the end, so visitors and Google never see a half-built site.

Keeping Banks Business Engine alive

Because the Engine is a plugin and not part of the current theme, swapping themes doesn't remove it or its data. The only part tied to Elementor is how the chat box is placed on the homepage. In the new theme we rebuild that placement as a native component, so the hero demo ("Try it now") and the site-wide concierge both call the same Engine underneath. Its dashboard stays where it is in wp-admin.

The motion layer

The 1904 layer already has smooth scroll, a header that gathers into a floating island, a page-transition curtain, a custom cursor, magnetic buttons and scroll reveals. We bring that over and push further. GSAP's bonus plugins became free in 2025, so SplitText (letter-by-letter type), Flip (layout morphs), MorphSVG and DrawSVG are all on the table at no licence cost.

  • Motion is set up with HTML attributes such as data-reveal and data-magnetic, so any new page gets the effects without new code.
  • Easing and timing live as brand tokens next to the colours, so every animation feels like one family.
  • Anyone with "reduce motion" switched on gets the calm version. Phones with weak hardware get lighter effects.
  • Speed budget: the page is usable in under 2.5 seconds on a mid-range Android on 4G in Lagos, and the motion scripts load after the content. Badass is no good if it's slow on the phones our clients' customers use.
Your call: where Elementor fits You said Elementor is still an important concept. My recommendation: every core page is coded in the theme (that's where the crazy motion lives), and Elementor stays installed with a blank "Canvas" template so the team can spin up a campaign landing page in an afternoon without a developer. Blog articles move to the normal WordPress editor, which the theme styles automatically.

05 · Client portal

From "yes" to launch without a single WhatsApp file dump

The path a new client walks, start to finish. Steps 1 to 4 can run without anyone on the team lifting a finger, and the team can also create a portal by hand for clients who come in by referral.

  1. Accept the proposalThe client opens a proposal link, picks a payment plan and signs the agreement on screen.
  2. Pay the depositCard, bank transfer or USSD through Paystack. Payment confirmed means the portal builds itself.
  3. Portal is createdAccount, project, milestones and an upload checklist are generated from the service they bought. Login link arrives by email and WhatsApp.
  4. Onboarding questionnaireBusiness, audience, competitors, sites they like, logins we'll need. Saved as they go, so they can finish it later.
  5. Upload everythingGuided folders for logo, brand files, copy, photos, videos and documents, with a "still needed" list that ticks itself off.
  6. Kickoff callBooked from inside the portal, using the Engine's bookings.
  7. Watch it being builtLive progress, preview links, approvals at each milestone.
  8. Pay the balance and launchFinal invoice unlocks the handover pack: logins, training videos and files.

The file vault

  • Structured, not a dumping ground: Brand (logos, fonts, colours), Content (page copy, product lists), Media (photos, videos), Documents (CAC, policies), Access (logins).
  • Big files welcome: uploads go directly to cloud storage in resumable pieces, so a 2 GB video survives a network drop and the website itself never slows down. Clients can also paste a Google Drive or Dropbox link.
  • Logins stored safely: passwords go in an encrypted field (the same AES-256-GCM approach the Engine uses for API keys), shown only to assigned team members, and every view is logged.
  • Every file shows who uploaded it and when, has comments, and keeps old versions.

Progress tracking

  • A timeline of phases (Discovery, Design, Build, Content, Review, Launch) with a percentage and the date each one is due.
  • Each milestone has a preview link and an Approve button. Approval is recorded with a timestamp, which also ends "I never agreed to that" arguments.
  • Feedback can be pinned onto a screenshot of the exact spot on the page.
  • An activity feed of what the team did today, plus a "waiting on you" box when the client is the blocker.

06 · Retainer desk

Yes, build it, as a mode of the same portal

You asked me to judge this one. A retainer desk is worth it: it turns "Managed Technology from ₦150k a month" from a promise into something clients can see working, and it stops support requests arriving scattered across WhatsApp, calls and email. But it should not be a separate system. When a project launches and the client signs a retainer, their portal simply switches on a Support tab. One login, one history.

  • Raise a request by type (something broke, content update, new feature, question), with screenshots or a screen recording attached.
  • Response clock by plan, shown openly: "We'll respond within 4 working hours". The team view flags anything close to breaching.
  • Hours or credits used this month against the plan, so extra work becomes a quote, not a surprise.
  • Site health panel: uptime, last backup, plugin updates applied, speed score. Proof of what they're paying for, every day.
  • Monthly report sent automatically: what we fixed, what we improved, what we recommend next. That last line is where upsells come from.

07 · Shop

Sell themes and plugins with real licence keys

Selling software is different from selling shirts. Buyers need a licence key, automatic updates inside their own WordPress, renewals, and limits on how many sites a key covers. The Engine already has licensing built in, so this is familiar ground.

Your call: which engine runs the shop My recommendation is Easy Digital Downloads with its Software Licensing add-on, styled entirely by our theme and charging through Paystack. It was made for exactly this (keys, updates, renewals, site limits) and is lighter than WooCommerce, which is built for physical stock. WooCommerce is the alternative if you ever expect to sell physical products too.
  • Product pages with a live demo site, changelog, requirements and screenshots.
  • Buyers' licences, downloads and renewals sit in the same portal as project clients, under a Licences tab.
  • Pricing in ₦ for Nigerian buyers and USD for everyone else.
  • Free versions can go on WordPress.org and point back to the shop for Pro.

08 · Developer page

Banks Labs: the page our plugins link home to

Every theme and plugin we ship has an "Author URI" and "Plugin URI". Those point to bankssolution.ng/labs, which does three jobs:

  • Shows the catalogue: every theme and plugin, free and paid, with version, last update and active installs.
  • Supports users: documentation, changelogs, a known-issues list and a support form that lands in the retainer desk queue.
  • Sells custom development: "Need a plugin built for your business?" with examples like the 1904 car-wash system and the Engine, leading into the Start a project flow.

This page is also where developers and other agencies judge us, so it's the right place for a public build log and code-quality badges.
